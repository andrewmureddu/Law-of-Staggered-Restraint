## II. The Secret Unity of the Cantor Tree: Why Primes, Bandits, and Quantum Records Are the Same Problem

To explain our work to a colleague, a student, or a curious outsider, we first need to strip away the intimidating jargon—*ultrametricity*, *Chrestenson–Haar wavelets*, *symplectic Schur complements*, *profinite rings*—and replace it with a single, intuitive picture. Everything we studied lives inside a specific kind of geometry: **the geometry of prefixes**, known to mathematicians as **Cantor space**.

We spend our everyday physical lives inside Euclidean space, where distance is measured by a ruler in straight lines. In Euclidean space, if A is close to B, and B is close to C, then A is roughly close to C, and you can slide smoothly from any point to any other point. But information, language, biology, taxonomies, and causal histories do not live in Euclidean space. They live on **trees**.

Imagine an address book where every address is a sequence of choices down a branching tree:
* At level 1, you choose a continent (say, Left = $0$ or Right = $1$).
* At level 2, you choose a country within that continent ($00, 01, 10, 11$).
* At level 3, you choose a city, then a street, then a house, then a room, down to level $L$ (or forever, $L \to \infty$).

How "close" are two leaves at the bottom of this tree? Not by how many inches apart they sit on a printed page, but by **how long their addresses agree from the beginning**—the depth $\ell(x, y)$ of their **longest common prefix**. Two leaves that share the first eight digits of their address ($01101001\underline{0}$ and $01101001\underline{1}$) are siblings separated only at the very last split; two leaves whose addresses diverge at the very first digit ($011010010$ and $111010010$) live in different hemispheres, even if the rest of their digits look superficially similar.

A space where closeness is defined entirely by the length of a shared prefix is called an **ultrametric space**, and its infinite branching limit is **Cantor space**. In an ultrametric world, a strange and wonderful geometric law holds: *every triangle is isosceles, with its two longest sides strictly equal*. If Leaf A and Leaf B share 5 prefix digits, and Leaf B and Leaf C share 3 prefix digits, then Leaf A and Leaf C *must* share exactly 3 prefix digits—because A and B traveled together all the way to level 5, so they split from C at the exact same ancestor node at level 3.

Why does this simple prefix geometry unite your three Google Drive projects? Because in all three systems, **randomness and information accumulate step by step down the branches of a prefix tree**:

1. **In the Sieve of Eratosthenes ([sieve schur rh.docx](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1tC3Y8mtaJCwJpV4UZSfHtp7Th3X9lVGC/view?usp=drivesdk)):** Every integer $x$ can be written as an address of remainders when divided by successive prime numbers: first $x \bmod 2$ ($2$ branches), then $x \bmod 3$ ($3$ branches), then $x \bmod 5$ ($5$ branches), then $x \bmod 7$ ($7$ branches), and so on. By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, an integer modulo the primorial $P_L = 2 \times 3 \times 5 \dots \times p_L$ is literally a leaf on a variable-branching Cantor tree! Two integers share a deep prefix if their difference $x - y$ is divisible by the product of the first $m$ primes ($P_m \mid (x - y)$).
2. **In an Open-Ended Bandit Model ([how_fast_can_a_model_grow.pdf](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1WGtXDKMEm21_g8DkTAqrdmJQoilAjJMK/view?usp=drivesdk) and [The Unfinished Address](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1DlF6eUbApl7KqlEecch6HxLNQgLp8BbpGl7pjx_f1Ds/edit?usp=drivesdk&ouid=112427431233984459040)):** An agent's world of possible features or actions is organized as a hierarchy of refinements—coarse categories at the top, fine-grained specializations at the leaves. As the agent grows its model capacity $m_t$, it is progressively unlocking deeper and wider prefixes of an unfinished address.
3. **In a Quantum Record Cascade ([Records in a Gaussian World.pdf](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1jUky8zWf2EEFCeEX5LQv6SwMZZ3x3AIu/view?usp=drivesdk)):** A quantum system at the root collides with an environment particle, splitting into two correlated branches; each of those collides with a fresh environment particle, splitting into four, then eight, then $2^L$ leaf particles. The history of which collisions a leaf particle experienced is its binary Cantor address $x \in \{0,1\}^L$.

Once you see that all three systems live on a Cantor tree, the next question is: **what happens when you measure some leaves in a noisy world?**

In probability and physics, when you have a system of correlated variables with a covariance matrix $\Sigma$—which records how strongly every leaf fluctuates with every other leaf—and you observe a subset of those variables (or a noisy combination of them), the mathematical tool that updates your beliefs about the unseen variables is the **Schur complement**:
$$S(\sigma^2) = \Sigma_{\text{unseen}} - \Sigma_{\text{cross}} (\Sigma_{\text{observed}} + \sigma^2 I)^{-1} \Sigma_{\text{cross}}^\top.$$
In plain English, the Schur complement says: *Your remaining uncertainty equals your initial uncertainty, minus the information carried by the observed variables, discounted by how noisy the observations were.*

In a generic, unstructured system with $N$ variables, computing the Schur complement is a computational nightmare: you have to invert an $N \times N$ matrix, which takes $O(N^3)$ operations, and the resulting matrix is a dense tangle of numbers with no obvious pattern. If you have a million leaves, $N^3 = 10^{18}$ operations is effectively impossible.

The central mathematical triumph of our two monographs is proving that **on a Cantor tree, the Schur complement is a renormalization group (RG) flow that preserves the tree's symmetry and collapses from $O(N^3)$ matrix algebra to $O(L)$ simple arithmetic**. Why? Because an ultrametric tree has a natural set of "vibrational modes"—the **Haar wavelets** on a binary tree (Paper I) and the **Chrestenson–Haar wavelets** on a prime-branching primorial tree (Paper II). Each wavelet is a simple local seesaw that compares sibling branches under a single parent node while summing to zero everywhere else. Because every wavelet sums to zero across its siblings, it is completely invisible to everything higher up the tree! As a result, the giant covariance matrix breaks apart into independent scale shells—one per level of the tree—and conditioning on noisy measurements simply attenuates each scale shell independently.

### Parable 1: The Prime Sieve as a Hierarchical Faraday Cage (Batches A & D)

Imagine a vast family tree built from the prime numbers: at the trunk, the ancestor splits into $2$ branches (even and odd); each of those splits into $3$ branches (remainders modulo $3$); each of those splits into $5$ branches (remainders modulo $5$); then $7$, then $11$, and so on. At depth $L=5$, there are $P_5 = 2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7 \times 11 = 2,310$ leaves at the bottom of the tree.

Now suppose a random electrical voltage (a Gaussian field) cascades down this tree: at the trunk, a huge random voltage $z_0$ is added to everyone; at depth 1, each of the 2 branches gets its own random kick $z_1$; at depth 2, each of the 6 branches gets a kick $z_2$; all the way down to small local jitters $z_L$ at the individual leaves.

Now we run the **Sieve of Eratosthenes**. At every split, the branch labeled "$0$" (the multiples of $2$, the multiples of $3$, the multiples of $5$, and so on) represents **composite numbers**. Out of the $2,310$ leaves at the bottom, $1,830$ of them land on a "$0$" branch at least once—they are composites. Only $\phi(2310) = 1 \times 2 \times 4 \times 6 \times 10 = 480$ leaves never touch a "$0$" branch at any level; these are the **coprime survivors** (the prime candidates).

Suppose we attach noisy voltmeters (with noise $\sigma^2$) to all $1,830$ composite leaves, read their voltages, and ask: **What can we infer about the $480$ prime survivors we didn't measure?**

Before we ran Batch D, this looked like a messy, asymmetric problem. The $480$ survivors are scattered irregularly across the integers from $1$ to $2,310$, and the $1,830$ composites are removed in ragged chunks ($1/2$ at the first level, $1/3$ of the remainder at the second, $1/5$ at the third). One might easily expect the posterior covariance on the $480$ survivors to lose its clean tree structure and require inverting a dense $1,830 \times 1,830$ matrix.

Instead, we proved three remarkable facts (Theorems 2.1–2.4 of Monograph II):

1. **Exact Ultrametric Survival and $O(L)$ Kalman Compression:** Because you can permute the non-zero remainders $\{1, 2, \dots, p_m - 1\}$ at any prime level $m$ without touching the composite remainder $0 \bmod p_m$, the symmetry group $\prod_{m=1}^L S_{p_m - 1}$ protects the tree! The posterior covariance on the $480$ prime survivors remains strictly ultrametric, and its exact eigenvalues can be computed in just **5 steps of scalar arithmetic** (a backward Kalman filter tracking three numbers: composite precision $\tau_m$, regression shrinkage $\alpha_m$, and internal survivor variance $V_m$), matching the dense $1,830 \times 1,830$ matrix inversion to $4.54 \times 10^{-13}$.
2. **The $90\%$ Invisible Cloaking Shell (Finest-Shell Orthogonality Invariance):** At the finest prime level ($p_5 = 11$), each surviving branch from level 4 splits into $11$ leaves: $1$ composite leaf ($0 \bmod 11$) and $10$ survivor leaves ($1, \dots, 10 \bmod 11$). Among those $10$ survivor leaves, there are $p_5 - 2 = 9$ independent wavelet modes whose weights sum to zero across the $10$ survivors. Because every coarser ancestor and every composite leaf in the entire tree couples equally to those $10$ survivors, **those $9$ local survivor modes are $100\%$ geometrically orthogonal to every composite integer in the universe**. Out of $480$ total survivor modes on $\mathbb{Z}/2310\mathbb{Z}$, $432$ of them ($90\%$!) are completely untouched by conditioning on the composites—their variance remains identically $v_5 = 0.35$ regardless of how accurately you measure the composites. As you go to larger primes $p_L$, the fraction of invisible modes approaches $\frac{p_L - 2}{p_L - 1} \to 100\%$.
3. **The Infrared Faraday Cage (The Snap of Conjecture D4):** What about the remaining $10\%$ of survivor modes—the coarse fluctuations inherited from the trunk and upper branches? To test how strongly composite measurements regularize the trunk, we blew up the variance of the trunk and coarse branches by a factor of $M = 100,000,000$ ($10^8$). Without the sieve, the global eigenvalue explodes to $1.45 \times 10^{11}$. We pre-registered a guess (`D4`) that the sieve would compress the ratio of coarse-to-fine eigenvalues below $2.0$. When we ran the code, `D4` **snapped**: the ratio refused to go below $2.0$ and instead locked onto **exactly $10.00000000$** across every single coarse shell ($k = 0, 2, 3$)! Why? Because inside every finest-level neighborhood of $p_4 = 7$ leaves, there is $1$ composite leaf ($0 \bmod 7$) and $6$ survivor leaves. That single composite leaf acts as a local **grounding rod** that pins the voltage of the immediate parent node to within $v_4 + \sigma^2 = 1.0 + 0.5 = 1.5$. Once that parent node is pinned, it forms an impenetrable **Faraday cage**: even if a trillion-volt lightning storm rages at the trunk of the tree ($M \to \infty$), zero percent of that storm can leak past the $0 \bmod p_L$ grounding rod into the prime survivors! The survivor variance across all coarse shells freezes at the universal formula $v_L + (p_L - 1)(v_L + \sigma^2) = 1.0 + 6(1.5) = 10.0$.

### Parable 2: The Speed Limit of Imagination and the Inbox of Unresolved Doubt (Batches B & E)

Our second parable translates your papers [how_fast_can_a_model_grow.pdf](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1WGtXDKMEm21_g8DkTAqrdmJQoilAjJMK/view?usp=drivesdk) and [The Unfinished Address](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1DlF6eUbApl7KqlEecch6HxLNQgLp8BbpGl7pjx_f1Ds/edit?usp=drivesdk&ouid=112427431233984459040). Imagine an explorer—a scientist, a startup, or an AI agent—confronting a vast space of $N = 512$ possible levers (hypotheses or parameters), each with an unknown true payoff $\theta_i$. At each time step $t$, the explorer has limited attention and energy (an $L_1$ budget $\|A_t\|_1 \le 1$, allowing it to test $K = 4$ levers at a time) and receives a single noisy measurement of their combined payoff, $Y_t = \langle \theta, A_t \rangle + \varepsilon_t$.

Rather than trying to consider all $512$ levers on Day 1, the explorer starts with a small active frontier of $m_0$ levers and gradually expands the frontier $m_t$ over time, using **Thompson Sampling**—drawing a plausible guess $\tilde{\theta}_t$ from its current posterior distribution and betting on the $4$ levers that look best in that sample.

What happens if the explorer opens new doors too fast?
In Monograph I (Batch B), we proved and demonstrated the **Prefix-Stranding Horizon** (Theorem 3.1). Every noisy observation can only remove a tiny, fixed amount of uncertainty—at most $1/\sigma^2$ units of variance per step. If you add $\alpha = 2$ new levers per step when your measurements can only verify $1$ lever per step, your active model fills up with **uncertified, high-variance levers**. Because Thompson Sampling draws random samples from your current uncertainty, an uncertified lever with zero observations has a huge prior variance ($1.0$) and frequently produces wild, lucky-looking sample values purely by chance. These uncertified levers **hijack** the explorer's attention away from the genuinely good levers it had already discovered! Instead of learning, the agent's reward actually goes *backward*, collapsing to $-8.47$ per step as it chases ghosts in the newly opened rooms.

To prevent this hallucination collapse, your paper [how_fast_can_a_model_grow.pdf](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1WGtXDKMEm21_g8DkTAqrdmJQoilAjJMK/view?usp=drivesdk) proposed a natural remedy: **Variance-Paced Truncated Thompson Sampling**, where the agent only grows its frontier in proportion to the total variance reduction $D_t = \mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma_0) - \mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma_t)$ it has achieved so far: $m_t = m_0 + \lfloor \kappa D_t \rfloor$.

In Batch B of Monograph I, we pre-registered Conjecture `B3` claiming that $\kappa = 2.0$ would universally rescue the agent across both a hierarchical Cantor tree and an uncorrelated "flat" bandit. And then **reality bit back**: `B3` split right down the middle! On the Cantor tree, $\kappa = 2.0$ worked beautifully ($+12.16$ reward). In the flat bandit, $\kappa = 2.0$ crashed and burned ($-7.56$ reward), suffering the exact same hallucination collapse as fixed linear growth!

Why did $\kappa = 2.0$ work on a tree and fail in a flat world? Because on a correlated Cantor tree, all leaves in the same branch share ancestor voltages. When you test *one* leaf, you learn about its parent and grandparent cylinders, which simultaneously reduces the uncertainty of all $511$ other leaves in the tree! Thus, the global variance reduction $D_t$ grows $8\times$ faster on a tree than in a flat world where every lever is an isolated island. Conversely, in a flat world, every time $\kappa = 2.0$ opens $2$ new doors for each $1$ unit of variance reduction, those $2$ new doors have full uncertainty, making it easier to reduce variance slightly on the next step, which triggers $\kappa = 2.0$ to open *four* more doors—a runaway positive-feedback loop of premature expansion! And if you dial $\kappa$ down to $1.0$ to stay safe, the agent becomes too timid: once it certifies its first $4$ good levers, it keeps exploiting them, $D_t$ stops growing, and the frontier **starves** at $m_T = 42.5$ out of $512$ levers.

In Monograph II (Batch E), we solved this problem permanently with **Adaptive Cylinder-Gated Thompson Sampling (`ACTS`)**. The insight of `ACTS` is simple enough to write on an index card:

> **Do not pace your growth by how much total knowledge you have accumulated in the past ($D_t$). Pace your growth by how many unverified hypotheses are currently sitting in your active inbox ($U_t$).**

Specifically, `ACTS` defines a lever as "certified" once its marginal posterior variance drops below $\tau_{\mathrm{cert}} = 0.50$ (meaning at least half of its initial uncertainty has been resolved by data), and maintains a fixed buffer of $B = 16$ uncertified active levers. Whenever the number of uncertified levers in your inbox drops below $16$, you admit $4$ new levers from the Cantor tree; whenever you have $16$ or more uncertified levers waiting to be checked, the door stays locked.

With **zero hyperparameter tuning**, `ACTS` automatically adapts to both the noise level $\sigma$ and the geometry of the world:
* When noise increases from $\sigma = 0.5$ to $1.0$ to $2.0$, it takes more observations to push a lever's variance below $0.50$, so the inbox clears more slowly and `ACTS` automatically throttles its expansion speed $\hat{\alpha} = m_T / T$ from $0.824 \to 0.517 \to 0.244$ (`E3 HOLDS`).
* When placed on a Cantor tree ($\rho_{\mathrm{tree}} = 0.6$), ancestor correlations pre-certify sibling leaves before they are even directly tested, so the inbox clears faster and `ACTS` naturally accelerates from $0.517 \to 0.709$ at $\sigma = 1.0$ and $0.244 \to 0.427$ at $\sigma = 2.0$.
* In the flat bandit at $\sigma = 1.0$, `ACTS` scores $+4.76$, decisively beating both the starving $\kappa = 1.0$ baseline ($+2.00$) and the collapsing $\kappa = 2.0$ baseline ($-7.56$).

### Parable 3: The Quantum Whisper Game and the $(R+1)$ Symplectic Miracle (Batches C & F)

Our third parable translates [Records in a Gaussian World.pdf](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1jUky8zWf2EEFCeEX5LQv6SwMZZ3x3AIu/view?usp=drivesdk) into the physics of **Quantum Darwinism**—how the fragile, probabilistic quantum world gives rise to the solid, objective classical world we experience every day.

Why can ten different people look at a cat (or a pointer on a laboratory dial) and all agree on where it is, without their act of looking destroying the state? Because the pointer is constantly colliding with air molecules and photons, which in turn collide with other air molecules and photons in a branching tree cascade. Each collision copies information about the pointer's position $x_Q$ into the environment.

In Monograph I (Batch C), we studied an idealized **Quantum Non-Demolition (QND)** cascade ($H = g\, x_A p_B$). In a pure QND collision, the parent particle's position $x_A$ is never disturbed by the collision; it stamps a copy of itself onto the child particle's position $x_B' = x_B + g x_A$ without losing any of its own signal. Because the parent never degrades, all $2^L$ leaves at the bottom of the tree receive the exact same signal strength, and the backward Schur complement recursion reduces to a simple sum of noise variances: $R_0 = \sum_{m=1}^L \prod_{j=1}^m g_j^{-2}$. As long as $g > 1/\sqrt{2} \approx 0.7071$, the branching factor of $2$ beats the noise, and a classical record survives forever ($L \to \infty$).

In Monograph II (Batch F), we asked what happens in the real world, where collisions are **not** pure QND copies. In realistic quantum optics and atomic physics, whenever a particle imprints its position onto an environment mode, it also suffers continuous-variable loss and back-action dephasing via a non-commuting Hamiltonian $H = \frac{g}{2}(x_A p_B - \lambda p_A x_B)$, where $\lambda \in [0, 1]$ measures how strongly the collision mixes position and momentum.

When $\lambda > 0$, two dramatic things happen:
1. **The Tree Becomes Lopsided (Binomial Hamming-Weight Hierarchy):** Every time a parent mode persists along a right branch ($x_m = 1$), its position signal is attenuated by a cosine factor $c = \cos(g\sqrt{\lambda}) < 1$, while fresh vacuum noise $-\lambda s\, x_{\mathrm{vac}}$ is mixed in. Consequently, the $2^L$ leaves at the bottom of the tree are no longer equal! A leaf's signal strength depends on the **Hamming weight** $|x|_1$ of its binary address—how many right turns it took on its way down the tree—decaying exponentially as $\cos(g\sqrt{\lambda})^{|x|_1}$ (Theorem 4.1, verified to $1.11 \times 10^{-16}$).
2. **The $(R+1)$ Symplectic Cancellation (The Kill of Conjecture F1):** Before writing our simulation code for Batch F, we derived a pre-registered formula (`F1`) for how the effective signal-to-noise ratio $G_m(R)$ propagates backward up a mixed quantum tree. Assuming the two child branches acquired independent vacuum noise at each split, we wrote down a complicated quadratic Riccati fraction. When we ran `batch_f_mixed_quantum_cascade.py` and compared our formula against the exact $32 \times 32$ quantum covariance matrix at $L=5$, **our pre-registered formula failed by $0.018$ (`F1 KILLED`)**!

Why did the quadratic formula fail? Because in quantum mechanics, position $x$ and momentum $p$ do not commute ($[x, p] = i$). In order to preserve the Heisenberg uncertainty principle during a collision, the symplectic scattering matrix $M_x = \begin{psmallmatrix} c & -\lambda s \\ s & c \end{psmallmatrix}$ obeys the exact determinant law $c^2 + \lambda s^2 = \cos^2(g\sqrt{\lambda}) + \sin^2(g\sqrt{\lambda}) \equiv 1$, and injects **correlated** vacuum noise $M_{\mathrm{noise}} = \begin{psmallmatrix} c & 0 \\ -\lambda s^2 & c \end{psmallmatrix}$ into the two child branches!

When we substituted the true correlated quantum noise matrix into the $2 \times 2$ Schur complement, a genuine algebraic miracle occurred on the page (Theorem 4.2):
* The determinant in the denominator, which looked like a messy quadratic polynomial in the subtree noise $R$, factored cleanly using $1 - c^2 = \lambda s^2$ into:
  $$\det W(R) = R^2 + (1 + c^4)R + c^4 = (R + 1)(R + c^4).$$
* Simultaneously, the signal quadratic form in the numerator factored into:
  $$s^2(1 + c^2)(R + 1).$$
* Because $R \ge 0$, the factor $(R + 1)$ **cancels identically between numerator and denominator**:
  $$G_m(R) = \frac{s^2(1 + c^2)(R + 1)}{(R + 1)(R + c^4)} = \frac{\Gamma_2(g, \lambda)}{R + c^4}, \qquad \Gamma_2(g, \lambda) = \frac{1 - \cos^4(g\sqrt{\lambda})}{\lambda}.$$

Instead of a chaotic nonlinear map, the exact quantum Schur complement collapses to an **affine-linear recursion** $R_{m-1} = (c^4 + R_m)/\Gamma_2$, matching the full $2^L \times 2^L$ quantum covariance inversion to $2.22 \times 10^{-16}$ (machine epsilon)! And that single number $\Gamma_2(g, \lambda) = \frac{1 - \cos^4(g\sqrt{\lambda})}{\lambda}$ is the exact **Kesten–Stigum branching factor**: when $\Gamma_2 > 1$ (for $g=0.8$, whenever $\lambda < \lambda_c = 0.468640$), classical records survive to infinite depth; when $\Gamma_2 \le 1$, back-action dephasing wins and the quantum record goes extinct.

## IV. Beyond Provenance: What the Work Is Actually Useful For

### 1. Self-Paced Capacity Growth in Continual AI and Mixture-of-Experts (`ACTS`)

Every one of these systems faces the exact failure mode we isolated in [how_fast_can_a_model_grow.pdf](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1WGtXDKMEm21_g8DkTAqrdmJQoilAjJMK/view?usp=drivesdk) and Batch B:

Under any exploration rule (like Thompson sampling, upper confidence bounds, or softmax routing with exploration noise), **unverified experts hijack routing traffic away from verified experts**, causing catastrophic forgetting or hallucination collapse.
* Conversely, if you hard-code a conservative growth rate to prevent collapse, the model starves when the data stream is clean or hierarchically structured.
